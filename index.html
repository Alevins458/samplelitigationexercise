INDEPENDENT PARALEGAL SKILLS PORTFOLIO · SIMULATED CASE STUDY

From source documents to litigation work product.

This portfolio documents an independent practical exercise I developed to familiarize myself with the responsibilities of a legal assistant or paralegal. Working from a fictional commercial dispute, I organized and assessed source records, prepared an evidence index and case chronology, drafted a case memorandum, and assembled a simulated litigation calendar.

The exercise builds on my prior experience supporting litigation-related expert research and professional coordination. It complements my study of e-discovery workflows, including my Relativity Review Pro certification preparation, and my Colorado notary public credential. The materials are training samples, not work performed for a real client or law firm.

STAGE 01 · FICTIONAL ATTORNEY ASSIGNMENT

The case and the work requested

Scenario: Horizon Manufacturing v. Summit Components

Horizon Manufacturing, LLC agreed to purchase 2,000 SC-40 control modules from Summit Components, Inc. for $84,000, with delivery due February 15, 2026. The shipments arrived late. Summit points to parts shortages and a subsequent labeling request; Horizon disputes the cause of delay and sought substitute goods. Questions remain about replacement costs, the disposition of 40 units flagged for inspection, and an unpaid invoice.

For the simulated litigation phase, a fictional Denver firm represents Horizon, the plaintiff, in an assumed Colorado state-court breach-of-contract action. No actual lawsuit, docket, or court order exists.

RolePlaintiff-side litigation supportSource file15 fictional documentsWork productFour deliverables

Hypothetical supervising attorney's instructions

“Review the supplied contract, correspondence, operations reports, shipping records, and accounting documents. Prepare work that allows counsel to evaluate the sequence of events, locate the supporting documents, identify factual conflicts, and coordinate the next steps in litigation.”

  1. Chronology: Extract significant dated events and cite each supporting record.
  2. Evidence index: Inventory and identify the underlying documents and custodians.
  3. Case memorandum: Summarize material facts, competing positions, evidentiary gaps, and follow-up.
  4. Calendar: Organize assumed litigation dates and preparation responsibilities; distinguish hypothetical milestones from actual rule-based obligations.

STAGE 04 · WHAT THIS DEMONSTRATES

Practical skills behind the finished documents

My aim was to practice the disciplined, traceable workflow required in legal support: review the record, distinguish documented facts from allegations, connect each assertion to its source, flag missing evidence, and communicate efficiently with supervising counsel.

Source-based analysis

Chronological reconstruction, supporting citations, contradictory accounts, and factual uncertainty.

Document management

Consistent document identifiers, custodian fields, searchable inventory, and linked originals.

Attorney communication

A concise case memorandum that separates record-supported facts, party positions, and follow-up requests.

Deadline awareness

An illustrative case calendar that models assignment tracking and reinforces the need to verify rules, triggers, and court orders.

Professional context: This self-directed work complements my experience in litigation-focused expert research and my continued professional development in e-discovery and Colorado notarial practice. It is not a substitute for supervised case experience, and it does not imply access to confidential client files.
